                                                                                     I.    INTRODUCTION

                                                Quantum state tomography (QST) determines an unknown state by making measurements on
                                            identical copies with highly important applications in various areas of quantum technologies [1–3].
                                            QST and process estimation tasks for characterizing and reconstructing purposes generally require
                                            exponential amount of measurements O(22n ) with the number of qubits n without any knowledge
                                            about the state. Low-rank density matrices with rank r ≪ 2n approximating pure states are
                                            reconstructed with O(r 2n nc ) Pauli measurements for a constant c ∈ [2, 6] by exploiting compressive
                                            sensing (CS) with convex or non-convex programming approaches [4–6] and experimental studies
                                            [7]. Linear optical methods are already utilized such as in [8] for multi-mode multi-photon states
                                            where finite number of linear optical interferometer configurations are used. The       reconstruction
                                            method requires ♯P-hard matrix permanent calculations with O poly(Dn,M , 2n ) complexity where

    There are diverse number of studies utilizing quantum CS or alternative approaches in more
practical manners for further reducing the number of measurements and the required resources
such as online learning and shadow tomography [11, 12], self-calibrating quantum state tomogra-
phy by relaxing the blind tomography problem to sparse de-mixing [13] and hierarchical compressed
sensing [14], adaptive compressive tomography without a-priori information [15], reduced density
matrices [16, 17], matrix product state tomography [2, 18], neural network [19], machine learn-
ing [20] based approaches or different methods including [21]. There is not any polynomial-time
quantum algorithm or solution method available for exactly reconstructing K-sparse pure states
in (1), i.e., K-sparse vectors in dimension 2n , with neither quantum nor classical polynomial-time
resource complexity. It is an open issue to achieve L1 -norm minimization based QST of K-sparse
pure states with quantum algorithms of polynomial-time complexity.
    In this article, we provide an alternative quantum polynomial-time solution withO(d K (logK)c )
measurement settings for K-sparse pure states in parallel with O c K log(2n / K) measurements
in classical CS without any complexity exponentially growing with 2n . On the other hand, the
computational complexity is also maintained as quantum polynomial-time providing a complete
practicality in QST tasks in analogy with the practical utilization of CS in classical world with
exponentially smaller dimensions compared with the Hilbert space size of quantum states. We
provide the locations of the K-sparse points without solving any L1 -norm minimization problem
with a different perspective compared with conventional CS. Phase estimation presents a new
approach for QST of pure states while encouraging the design of new unitary operators UΦ       ~ with
the proposed eigenstructure and practical implementation capability. It is an open issue to realize
polynomial size quantum circuit implementations for exponentially large powers of the presented
specific design based on linear optics and WPDs. Furthermore, analyzing the effects of noise in
input state and estimation errors are important for practical considerations [7, 22]. Extension to
mixed state inputs is also an open issue.
    The proposed architecture is promising to be utilized in all state estimation and process modeling
tasks [1–3, 11]. Quantifying the amount of entanglement existing in a quantum state is another
potential application [23]. Quantification generally requires full state tomography and complex
calculations for entanglement monotones [24]. Furthermore, the proposed method can be utilized
to map classical states into eigenvectors of UΦ  ~ for various machine learning procedures similar to
embedding into the amplitude, basis or the dynamics of quantum systems through Hamiltonian
embedding [25], or using various quantum feature map operators [26–28]. The embedded classical
data is extracted reliably in quantum polynomial-time by using the proposed QST architecture.
